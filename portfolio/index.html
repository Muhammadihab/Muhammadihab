case 02 · Logika

Zaboon

CRMFluttermobile + desktop5 attack paths closed

Most small shops in Egypt sell through WhatsApp. Customers ask "how much?", the message sinks under forty others, and nobody follows up.

Zaboon logs every customer who asks, where they came from, and when someone should call them back. The owner opens it in the morning and sees who to call today, the sales pipeline, money still owed, and which channel actually brings customers.

Security was designed in from the start, not added at the end. Before delivery I tested it as an attacker and closed 5 attack paths.

  • Today
  • Customers
  • Sales pipeline
  • Follow-ups
  • Reports
  • Team

04 / how a project goes

Four steps. The third is where I try to break it.

Written the way I write commits. Open the break step to see what I attack before handover.

git log --oneline your-app
a1c09e2talk
two lines on WhatsAppYou tell me what the app should do and who uses it. I reply the same day, and if you don't need an app, I'll say so.
4be17f0build
a working build on your phone, earlyNot slides. You install it, tap through it, and tell me what's wrong while it's still cheap to change.
9d3a5c1break
I attack it, you get the reportSeven checks on every app, plus whatever is specific to yours. Every finding is fixed before handover. Opened: 0/7

  1. I unpack my own release build the way an attacker would and look for API keys and database URLs that shouldn't ship. Release builds are obfuscated with --obfuscate --split-debug-info.

  2. Change /orders/1041 to /orders/1042 and get someone else's order. I try it on every endpoint. The server checks who's asking, not just whether they're logged in.

  3. Tokens go into the platform's secure storage (Keychain / Keystore), not plain preferences. A lost phone leaves nothing useful lying around.

  4. Hiding the delete button is design, not security. Every role is enforced on the server, so calling the API directly gets the same no.

  5. HTTPS everywhere, and certificate pinning when the data is worth intercepting. I watch what the app actually sends through a proxy.

  6. Who changed a price, who deleted an invoice, and when. Readable by admins, editable by nobody.

  7. Negative quantities, a 10,000-character name, a script inside a note field. Users will do it by accident. I do it first.
